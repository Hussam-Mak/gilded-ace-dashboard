# Luck, location, or a leak?

The general manager planned to replace the ten machines that earned the least. Before spending $220,000, we tested why each one earned so little.

**What we found**

- **Luck.** We scored how far each machine's win sat from what its bets predicted. Across 310 machines the scores match pure chance; nine of the bottom ten are ordinary luck or low play.
- **Location.** Nine of the bottom ten sit in Back Forty, where the same games earn 0.65× what they earn elsewhere. When its main walkway closed (Mar 3–Apr 6), Back Forty fell from 64% to 44% of the floor's rate while floor totals stayed on forecast: access moves play rather than creating it.
- **Leak.** M-1158 pays out on 38.6% of 674,454 spins against a published 31.5%, about 124 standard errors and steady every month. That is a reason to inspect, not proof of wrongdoing.
- **Downtime.** Repairs take about three hours everywhere; Ridgeline machines fault 3.6× as often as Halcyon's, and waits spike when scheduled technicians are absent.
- **Players and comps.** Card holders lost about what their bets predicted; the top 10% generate 66% of expected loss. On May 1 every comp became about 53% cheaper in points, raising comp cost with no measurable gain in play.

**Model**

A weekday-plus-calendar forecast of daily coin-in, trained on January–April and frozen before scoring May–June, misses by $47K a day, against $69K for a weekday average and $218K for a flat mean. Used as a counterfactual, it shows February's slot tournament added $566K–$884K of net wagers against a pre-event baseline, beating all 20 ordinary weekends.

**July plan: $16,200 committed, $83,800 kept**

1. **Inspect M-1158 ($1,200).** Success: a documented paytable reconciliation, then a three-day hit rate within 30.6–32.4%.
2. **One tournament weekend, July 18–20 ($15,000),** ordered by July 4 only if go/no-go checks pass. Break-even is $158K of extra wagers; success is a lift above $212K against the frozen forecast, measured through July 27 to catch displaced play.

If the checks fail, we spend $1,200 and keep $98,800. Replacements, moves, signage and an extra technician were not funded: none would pay off within July.

**Limits.** Synthetic data; figures are expected house win, not realized profit; the promotion evidence rests on one event.
