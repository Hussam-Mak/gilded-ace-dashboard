Question 2 · What changed in demand, and why?

Demand is steady and weekly. Where a machine sits matters more than which machine it is.

Saturdays bring in about 60% more than Tuesdays, and citywide events add more. Back Forty machines earn about 0.65× what the same game earns elsewhere. When its main walkway closed for renovation, that zone fell further while the rest of the floor picked up the play.

Question 3 · How much play was lost to downtime, and when?

Repairs take about three hours. What varies is how often machines break and how long they wait.

Ridgeline machines fault several times as often per machine as Halcyon's. Waits are a queue: with two technicians on shift a new fault is picked up at once unless two others are open. Short-staffed shifts in May doubled the play lost while waiting.

How the model changes a decision

  • It is the counterfactual for the promotion: actual coin-in minus the frozen forecast gives the lift.
  • Using only data before the event, the February tournament produced $566K–$884K of net extra wagers, against a $158K break-even for a $15,000 package.
  • On 20 ordinary weekends the same method finds deviations from −$300K to +$212K, so a July lift above $212K stands out from all of them.

Question 6 · The $100,000 decision for July 1–30

Inspect the one leak. Run one tournament if it passes its checks. Keep the rest.

$16,200 committed, $83,800 kept. If any promotion check fails on July 4, spend $1,200 and keep $98,800. Each action links to the evidence behind it.

Payout · $1,200 · order Jul 1, inspect Jul 2

Inspect and reconcile M-1158

  • Hits 38.57% of 674,454 spins vs 31.54% on the other 47 Desert Gold machines, every month.
  • Buys information: paytable, software, meters and jackpot accounting. Any repair is separate and unscoped.
  • If a correction follows, about $200 a day of expected house win returns, so it pays back in about six days.
  • Success: a documented reconciliation, then a three-day hit rate inside 30.6%–32.4%.
Demand · $15,000 · order by Jul 4 · run Jul 18–20

One tournament-style promotion weekend

  • February's tournament: $566K–$884K net extra wagers against a pre-event baseline. Break-even: $158K ($211K with $5K of extra costs).
  • Measured against the frozen forecast for July 18–27, including the following week for displaced play.
  • Counts as clearly above noise if the weekend alone exceeds $212K.
  • A second weekend would have to be ordered before the first runs, so it is not funded.

Go/no-go rules, checked by July 4

  1. February lift under the stricter baseline is at least twice break-even ($317K). Currently $566K: passes.
  2. The package covers prizes, marketing and staffing; costs outside it are $5,000 or less.
  3. No citywide event or holiday on July 18–20, so the forecast measures the weekend cleanly.
  4. Open to all guests, with no extra offers aimed at the heaviest-losing players.

If any rule fails: inspection only, keep $98,800.

Not funded

  • Replace the bottom ten: nine are within normal luck and nine sit in Back Forty. Buying ten costs $220,000.
