The manager's plan

Replacing the ten lowest earners would cost $220,000 and target the wrong cause

  • 9 of the 10 earned what luck predicts for the bets they took. Across all 310 machines the luck scores form the bell curve chance alone produces.
  • 9 of the 10 sit in Back Forty, where the same games earn 0.65× what they earn elsewhere on the same day. A new machine in the same slot would face the same traffic.
  • Three were on the floor for only 49 to 132 days, so six-month totals understate them.
  • The one machine on the list that fails the luck test, M-1158, calls for an inspection, not a new cabinet.

The two funded actions

Payout · 1 inspection

Inspect and reconcile M-1158

Strong evidence of a problem$1,200
Evidence

A 38.57% hit rate across 674,454 spins, against 31.54% for the other 47 machines on the same Desert Gold configuration. The gap is about 124 standard errors and holds in every month. Hold ran about 3% against a configured 10%.

What the $1,200 buys

Information. The inspection identifies whether the installed paytable, software, meters or jackpot accounting differ from the published sheet. Any repair is separate: its price, timing and success are unknown.

Break-even

If a correction follows, expected house win rises by about $200 a day, so the inspection pays back in about six days. If nothing is found, it costs $1,200 and narrows the cause to accounting or meters.

Success criteria

A documented reconciliation within one day. After any correction, hit rate over the next three days falls within 30.6%–32.4% (±2 standard errors at about 3,700 spins a day). July hold is not a criterion: it swings too much month to month.

Sources: machine_day (paid_spins, payout_positive_spins, sum_bet_squared), game_math, placements, operations · analysis/checks.py

Demand · 1 weekend · conditional

One tournament-style promotion, Friday–Sunday July 18–20

Moderate evidence$15,000
Evidence

Against a baseline built only from January 1 – February 20, the February slot tournament ran $705K–$859K above expected over three days. The following week ran $139K below to $24K above. Net excess wagers: $566K–$884K. These are forecast deviations, not proof of cause.

Why the range

The low end uses every pre-event day, including CES, Super Bowl and holiday days, which raise the baseline. The high end uses ordinary pre-event days only. An earlier estimate of $803K used a baseline that included later months and is no longer used.

Break-even

At a 9.5% expected hold, the $15,000 package needs $158K of net extra wagers; with $5,000 of costs outside the package, $211K. The conservative February result is 3.6× the first figure.

Why these dates

July 18–20 is the first full Friday–Sunday after the 14-day lead, stays clear of July 4, and matches the February format. A second July weekend would have to be ordered before the first one runs, so it is deferred to August.

Go/no-go rules, checked by July 4
  1. The February lift under the stricter all-day baseline is at least twice break-even ($317K). Currently $566K: passes.
  2. The package price is confirmed to include prizes, marketing and staffing, with costs outside it estimated at $5,000 or less.
  3. No citywide event or holiday falls on July 18–20, so the frozen forecast measures the weekend cleanly.
  4. The promotion is open to all guests, with no extra offers aimed at the heaviest-losing players.

If any rule fails, take the inspection-only fallback.

Success criteria

Net extra wagers for July 18–27 against the frozen forecast exceed $158K (break-even). The lift counts as clearly above noise if the weekend alone exceeds $212K, the largest deviation among 20 ordinary weekends.

What gets reported

Property-wide wagers and expected house win against the forecast, realized house win, and contribution after the package, comp cost and food-and-drink cost of goods. Attendance alone is not a success measure.

Sources: property_day, calendar_day, events (announced_date ≤ forecast freeze) · analysis/forecast.py, analysis/checks.py, analysis/plan_options.py

Assumptions

  • All benefits are expected house win at the floor's 9.5% hold, minus catalog costs charged in full. Benefits count only for days after each lead time inside July 1–30.
  • The July forecast is refit on January–June and frozen on July 1, using only events announced by then. The after-the-fact lift for Locals Week (announced May 26) was never a forecast input.
  • Simulation inputs: promotion lift from a Locals Week-like result up to a repeat of February; inspection fix succeeds 50–100% of the time; Ridgeline fault cut 0–75%.
  • Costs outside the promotion package are capped at $5,000 in the scenarios. Comp cost already sits inside food-and-drink cost of goods and is counted once.
